# The 15-Minute Demo — Hotel Growth OS

**Rule zero: never show the settings screen.** Owners buy the front desk and the report. Everything else is noise.

**Before the call**
- Open the live system with a demo tenant seeded with realistic data (occupied rooms, a few dues, a returning guest). An empty system sells nothing.
- Have the prospect's hotel name and room count in front of you. Say them out loud during the demo.
- Screen share on WhatsApp video or Google Meet. In person is better — Dhaka, Cox's Bazar and Sylhet are all worth the trip for a 40+ room hotel.

---

## Minute 0–1 · Frame it

> "I am not going to walk you through features. I am going to check a guest in, take a payment, and then show you the report your accountant asks for. If it does not look better than what you do now, tell me and we will stop."

Then stop talking and start clicking.

---

## Minute 1–4 · A booking, end to end

1. **Create a reservation** — guest name, dates, room. Point out the room picker showing what is actually free.
2. **Check the guest in** — one button. Say: *"That is the whole check-in. One screen."*
3. **Add a charge** — a restaurant bill or laundry to the folio. Point out it attaches to the reservation, not the room.
4. **Take a partial payment** — pay half. Point out the balance updating.

**The line that lands:** *"Notice I never typed the guest's name twice."*

---

## Minute 4–7 · The room matrix

Open the room grid. Occupied, vacant, cleaning, maintenance — all live.

> "Your housekeeping supervisor sees this on her phone. When she marks 302 clean, your front desk sees it immediately. No phone call, no slip of paper."

Show the due-out list. *"This is the morning list — who is leaving today."*

---

## Minute 7–10 · The guest record

Search a returning guest. Show their stay history, previous rooms, notes, total lifetime spend.

> "When this guest walks in next time, whoever is at the desk knows they stayed with you three times and prefer a high floor. Right now that lives in your manager's head — and it leaves when he does."

---

## Minute 10–13 · The report (this is what closes)

Open the revenue and occupancy report.

> "Occupancy, ADR, revenue, outstanding dues. Any date range. Generated from what your front desk already typed — nobody does extra work to produce it."

Then run outstanding dues **as of a past date**.

> "This is the one most systems get wrong. This rebuilds the balance as it stood that day. Your accountant will care about this more than anything else I have shown you."

---

## Minute 13–15 · Close on a specific next step

Do not ask "what do you think?" Ask a decision question:

> "Two questions. One — is there anything here that would not work for how {{hotel_name}} runs? Two — if we did this, who besides you would need to say yes?"

Then propose the actual next step:

> "The way it normally goes: I come to your property, watch your front desk for an hour, and send you a written proposal with your exact numbers in it. That takes me a morning and costs you nothing. Can I do that this week?"

---

## What to do when they ask something you cannot do

Say so, immediately, and write it down in front of them.

> "No, that is not built. Let me write it down — you are the second person to ask."

You will win more deals from honest "no"s than from vague "yes"s. And the list becomes your roadmap.

---

## After the demo — same day

1. Log the demo in `/crm/growth` with outcome and next follow-up date.
2. Send the WhatsApp WA-3 follow-up.
3. If they asked for anything you could not do, add it to the feature request list with their hotel name attached.
4. If it is a real opportunity, create a **deal** record with plan, MRR and expected close date. If you would not put money on it closing, do not create the deal — an honest pipeline is worth more than a big one.
